## Partnership Models for Policy Research Uptake

![Featured image for this post](/pl/highlight-work.svg)

*Featured visual for this article.*

One major challenge in evidence-informed decision making is the disconnect between research production and policy timelines. Research may be rigorous, but if it reaches decision makers too late or in the wrong format, uptake remains low.

Uganda's emerging EIDM ecosystem points to a practical answer: structured partnerships between researchers, technical policy units, and implementation actors. Institutions such as ACRES show how this bridge can be built through continuous engagement rather than one-off dissemination.

## Core Elements of a Strong Partnership Model

A working partnership model usually includes:

- regular co-framing of policy questions
- shared expectations for timelines and outputs
- decision-ready evidence formats
- feedback loops after policy use

These elements ensure that evidence is both credible and usable in live policy processes.

## Operational Benefits

When partnerships are structured, ministries and district teams benefit from:

- faster access to contextualized evidence
- clearer option appraisal during planning
- better continuity between policy design and implementation

This makes EIDM less dependent on individual champions and more embedded in institutional workflows.

## Closing Note

Sustainable evidence use depends on relationships, not only reports. Building durable researcher-policy partnerships is one of the most practical paths to stronger policy uptake in Uganda.
