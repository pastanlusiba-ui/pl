## Embedding Evidence Briefs in District Planning Cycles

District planning meetings often include many competing priorities and limited decision time. A well-structured evidence brief can improve these discussions by giving decision makers one concise, comparable input for each policy option.

Across Uganda, institutions advancing evidence use have emphasized practical formatting: short summaries, clear options, implementation constraints, and cost implications. This structure helps planning teams avoid abstract debate and focus on feasible choices.

## What an Effective Brief Should Include

An evidence brief used in district workflows should cover:

- the decision question and timeline
- a short synthesis of relevant evidence
- two to three options with trade-offs
- implications for staffing, budget, and delivery

This is especially useful when district technical teams must align local priorities with national guidance in a short window.

## Workflow Integration Points

To make briefs useful, they should enter planning cycles at predictable points:

1. During pre-meeting technical preparation
2. In option appraisal discussions
3. In final recommendation notes to leadership

When these entry points are standardized, evidence use becomes routine instead of optional.

## Closing Note

EIDM improves when evidence products are designed for existing decision routines. District planning cycles are one of the strongest places to institutionalize that practice in Uganda.
