## From Evidence to Action in Uganda's Health System

Evidence-informed decision making (EIDM) is often described as a technical process, but in practice it is mostly an institutional process. In Uganda, this means connecting research producers, policy analysts, implementers, and frontline public servants in one decision cycle.

One useful lesson from ACRES is that EIDM works best when it is treated as a service to decision makers, not as a separate academic exercise. ACRES positions itself as a policy and implementation-focused evidence partner, with a strong emphasis on practical public health decisions and system improvement.

## Why This Matters

- Ministries and local governments often make decisions under time pressure.
- Technical reports are useful, but only if they are translated into timely options.
- Better evidence use can improve resource allocation, implementation choices, and accountability.

In other words, EIDM is about relevance, timing, and usability. The quality of evidence matters, but the quality of the decision interface matters just as much.

## A Practical Three-Step Approach

### 1. Define the Decision Clearly

Before requesting evidence, define:

- the policy question
- the decision deadline
- the implementation constraints

This keeps evidence products focused on a real choice, not just a broad topic.

### 2. Build Decision-Ready Products

Decision-ready products can include short evidence briefs, structured options, and implementation implications. The point is to present evidence in a format that can be used in real meetings and planning sessions.

### 3. Track Uptake and Learning

If we do not track what gets used, we cannot improve the system. Basic follow-up should capture:

- what recommendation was used
- what was adapted for context
- what implementation outcomes were observed

## Closing Note

Uganda's EIDM opportunity is not only to produce more evidence, but to strengthen how evidence enters decisions at national and local levels. ACRES' work on policy-facing evidence support is a strong reference for this direction.
