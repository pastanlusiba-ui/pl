## Why National Evidence Week Matters for Local Solutions

National events can either remain symbolic or become platforms that change practice. In Uganda, National Evidence Week has the potential to do the latter when it connects national dialogue with local implementation.

ACRES announced National Evidence Week under the theme of local solutions for local problems. That framing is important. It acknowledges that evidence is strongest when it is interpreted in context, co-owned by local institutions, and tied to feasible implementation pathways.

## Beyond the Event: What Should Change?

A meaningful Evidence Week should lead to changes in how institutions work after the event:

- Better routines for asking evidence-focused policy questions
- Stronger collaboration between researchers and public decision makers
- More use of concise briefs in planning, budgeting, and supervision cycles

If these changes happen, Evidence Week becomes a systems intervention, not just a calendar event.

## Three Priorities for Follow-Through

### 1. Institutionalize Evidence Use

Create standard points in policy and planning where evidence is required before major decisions are finalized.

### 2. Build Capacity in Real Workflows

Capacity building should be linked to real tasks: drafting options, reviewing budgets, improving implementation guidelines, and tracking outcomes.

### 3. Strengthen Local Government Linkages

National evidence conversations need district pathways. Local government teams should be active users and contributors, not passive recipients.

## Why This Matters for EIDM in Uganda

Evidence-informed decision making depends on culture as much as technical tools. Events such as National Evidence Week help shape that culture by making evidence use visible, expected, and collaborative.

Over time, this can shift institutions from ad hoc evidence use to routine evidence practice.

## Closing Note

The strongest EIDM systems are those that remain grounded in local decision problems. Uganda's evidence agenda is most effective when national leadership and local implementation are intentionally connected.
