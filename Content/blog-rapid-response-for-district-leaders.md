## Rapid Response Evidence for District Decision Makers

Many policy and implementation decisions cannot wait for long research cycles. District health teams, technical working groups, and ministry departments often need evidence in days, not months. This is where rapid response evidence support becomes valuable.

ACRES has highlighted this model through rapid response work with the Ministry of Health and selected local governments. Their update describes the delivery of multiple evidence products in one year, showing that high-frequency evidence support is both possible and useful in Ugandan policy environments.

## What Rapid Response Should Deliver

For decision makers, "rapid response" should not mean "low quality." It should mean:

- clearly framed decision questions
- concise synthesis of best available evidence
- direct implications for local implementation

The product should be short, practical, and tied to an actual policy or implementation window.

## A District-Facing Template

Below is a simple template for a rapid evidence note:

1. **Decision question:** What exactly needs to be decided?
2. **Context:** Which district realities matter most?
3. **Evidence summary:** What is known, and with what confidence?
4. **Options:** What can be done now, and what are trade-offs?
5. **Implementation notes:** Staffing, budget, and operational needs.

Using this template consistently helps teams compare decisions over time and improve institutional memory.

## Why This Matters for Uganda

Uganda's decentralized implementation structure means district teams need evidence that is usable in context, not generic recommendations. Fast, context-sensitive evidence products can help reduce delays between policy intention and practical execution.

Rapid response systems also create a feedback loop: policy teams ask better questions, and evidence teams produce better targeted outputs.

## Closing Note

EIDM becomes real when it supports real decisions at the speed those decisions are made. The rapid response model is one of the most practical ways to do this in Uganda.
